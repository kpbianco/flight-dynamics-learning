## Model boundary, equations, and recognizable outputs

The 13 numeric states support six physical degrees of freedom:

```text
x = [north, east, down, u, v, w, q0, q1, q2, q3, p, q, r]'

position_dot_n = C_body_to_ned velocity_b
velocity_dot_b = force_non_gravity_b/m
               + C_body_to_ned' [0;0;g]
               - omega_b cross velocity_b
omega_dot_b    = I \ (moment_b - omega_b cross (I omega_b))
quaternion_dot = 0.5 q_body_to_ned tensor_product [0;omega_b]
```

- Body axes are right-handed `x` forward, `y` right, `z` down; navigation is North-East-Down with Down positive. The scalar-first Hamilton quaternion and `C_body_to_ned` rotate body components into NED.
- Fixed teaching body: `m=1200 kg`, `I=diag([2500,3000,4000]) kg*m^2`, `g=9.80665 m/s^2`, initial NED origin, `v_b(0)=[60,0,0] m/s`, identity quaternion, and zero body rate.
- Non-gravity force is `[2400 force_scale h(t,1.5), 0, -m g]' N`; moment is `moment_scale [500,700,350]' h(t,1.0) N*m`; `h` is a finite half-sine reevaluated at every RK4 stage.
- Both pulse scales accept finite real scalars in `[0,1.5]`. The fixed `0:0.02:6 s` grid has 301 samples and 300 steps. Each normal or broken trajectory makes 1,200 derivative evaluations; each model call performs one of each synchronously.
- The model normalizes the quaternion used at every derivative stage and projects each complete RK4 update. Euler 3-2-1 angles are derived for presentation and are not propagated states.
- Independent baseline outputs: final NED position `[328.491610432, 28.829715915, 13.362100336] m`; final body velocity `[25.743967741, 18.058628363, 29.693106943] m/s`; final Euler angles `[53.021055871, 37.780743707, 37.440862000] deg`; final speed `43.249815414 m/s`; peak speed `61.331578859 m/s`; peak body-rate magnitude `11.652319550 deg/s`; peak shortest attitude rotation `63.957280876 deg`.
- After the one-second moment pulse, rotational kinetic energy and inertial angular momentum remain constant within the retained `1e-11` relative oracle bound.
- At zero force and moment pulse scales, the exact physical limit is `[north,east,down]=[60t,0,0] m`, constant `[60,0,0] m/s` body velocity, identity quaternion, and zero rates. The correct and broken trajectories coincide because `omega=0`.
- With force scale one and moment scale zero, analytic half-sine integration gives final forward speed `61.909859317 m/s` and North `370.026761415 m`; the fixed recurrence agrees within `2e-8`.
- The force sweep `[0,0.5,1,1.25,1.5]` changes only forward body force and translational states; quaternion, rates, and moment history remain identical. Final North and speed increase monotonically.
- The moment sweep `[0,0.5,1,1.25,1.5]` preserves the entire force history; peak rate and attitude rotation increase monotonically while the rotated body-fixed support force changes the NED path.
- The broken comparison omits only rotating-frame velocity transport. Baseline final path separation is `132.791783072 m`; peak complete-equation residual is `9.781530671 m/s^2`; quaternion, body rates, forces, and moments remain unchanged. These are defect symptoms, not aircraft predictions.
- The body, loads, and pulses are transparent teaching assumptions. There is no aerodynamic, actuator, sensor, Earth-rotation, identified-aircraft, handling-qualities, or certification fidelity.

### Independent system-risk review and retained-behavior repairs

The independent review found two medium-severity, in-scope defects. First, every finite moment scale
in `[0,1.5]` is accepted, but the model used `momentPulseScale==0` to decide whether normalized
post-pulse angular-momentum drift had a denominator. At the smallest positive double, the rotational
response rounds to zero while the input remains nonzero, so the prior code divided zero by zero and
returned `NaN` in an accepted result. The repair now measures the computed reference angular-momentum
norm and returns zero drift whenever that norm is zero. A retained MATLAB assertion calls
`model(1,eps(0))`; the independent Python boundary executes the equivalent `math.ulp(0.0)` behavior,
requires zero representable rate and finite zero drift, and static linkage rejects the input-based
guard.

Second, the lesson and evidence claimed post-pulse rotational-energy conservation within a retained
`1e-11` independent-oracle bound, but the Python suite checked only inertial angular momentum. The
unexecuted MATLAB check already contained the energy assertion. The new independent behavioral test
uses the nonzero baseline after torque removal, requires body-rate components to continue changing,
and bounds relative rotational-energy drift below `1e-11`; this prevents a frozen zero-motion case
from satisfying the claim trivially. No equation, load, public field, launcher, persistence format,
dependency, or asynchronous behavior changed.
